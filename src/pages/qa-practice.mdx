## 1. The project and its purpose

<PracticeQuestion q="In one or two sentences, what is LAFINA?" tip="Local AI Secretary. Companion + planner + calls. Offline core, optional online AI.">

LAFINA, short for **Local AI Secretary**, is an AI student companion for Android. It keeps a student's classes, tasks, notes, reminders, and alarms in one place, lets them manage everything by tapping, typing, or talking, chats with them, and calls them with a simulated phone call when a reminder is due.

</PracticeQuestion>

<PracticeQuestion q="What does the name LAFINA mean?" tip="Local AI Secretary. 'Local' = core works on the phone.">

LAFINA stands for **Local AI Secretary**. "Local" means its essential functions (planning, reminders, supported voice commands) run directly on the phone. "Secretary" means it doesn't just answer questions. It helps you get things done: it schedules, reminds, takes notes, and keeps you organized.

</PracticeQuestion>

<PracticeQuestion q="What problem does LAFINA solve?" tip="Scattered tools + alert fatigue + unreliable internet → one companion that calls you.">

Students juggle classes, deadlines, notes, and personal commitments across many apps, and ordinary push notifications are easy to swipe away (alert fatigue). Many students also have unreliable internet. LAFINA puts planning, reminders, school information, and an assistant in one app that works offline, and delivers important reminders as a call that the student answers and responds to.

</PracticeQuestion>

<PracticeQuestion q="What are the core features of LAFINA?" tip="Plan, remind (calls), talk, study, Ask USTP, remember.">

- Schedule, calendar, task, and note management (with COR import for USTP classes)
- Call-style reminders and alarms
- AI chat by text or voice: planning commands offline, open conversation online
- Call LAFINA: hands-free voice conversations
- Ask USTP: handbook and campus answers with sources
- Memory: personalized preferences
- Study skills: Pomodoro, flashcards, quizzes, GWA calculator, summarizer, recordings, documents

</PracticeQuestion>

<PracticeQuestion q="What is the main purpose of LAFINA?" tip="Help students stay organized, anywhere, with a companion they want to use.">

To help students manage their academic responsibilities more easily through one intelligent, accessible assistant. It works even without internet, and it's a companion students actually want to open every day.

</PracticeQuestion>

<PracticeQuestion q="Your earlier scope said LAFINA is not a chatbot. Now it chats and answers trivia. What changed?" level="deeper" tip="Started task-only; grew into a companion. Planner core and safety rules did not change.">

The original design was a closed-domain, task-only scheduler so that small on-device models could run it. During development we added optional online AI (DeepSeek), so with Online Mode LAFINA can hold a normal conversation, answer general and trivia questions, and help with studying. That makes it a student companion, not only a scheduler.

The core did not change. Planning still works offline, and any change to the student's data still goes through a review card that the student confirms.

</PracticeQuestion>

<PracticeQuestion q="Why is it called a secretary instead of just a chatbot?" tip="A chatbot talks; a secretary acts.">

A chatbot mainly responds to questions. LAFINA also **performs actions**: it creates schedules, sets reminders, manages tasks, saves notes, and calls you when something is due. It always shows the change for your approval first. It helps you accomplish tasks, not just get information.

</PracticeQuestion>

<PracticeQuestion q="Who are the target users?" tip="Students first; USTP-specific extras.">

University students in general, with extra features for USTP Cagayan de Oro students: importing a Certificate of Registration (COR) into the schedule, and **Ask USTP**, which answers handbook and campus questions with sources. It especially helps students with limited or unreliable internet.

</PracticeQuestion>

<PracticeQuestion q="Why did you choose students as your target users?" tip="Many deadlines, many apps, limited data.">

Students handle many classes, deadlines, exams, and activities at once, often across several apps, and many rely on limited mobile data. That makes them a group that benefits a lot from one offline-capable assistant. We are students ourselves, so we understand the problem first-hand.

</PracticeQuestion>

<PracticeQuestion q="What makes LAFINA innovative?" tip="Not new parts; a new combination built for students.">

The innovation isn't inventing each technology. It's **combining** them into one student-focused system:
- offline-first planning,
- proactive call-style reminders,
- AI that acts only after the student confirms,
- source-backed USTP answers,
- a companion personality.

We built this combination specifically for the student problem.

</PracticeQuestion>

<PracticeQuestion q="What is the most important feature of LAFINA?" tip="Offline-first planning + reminders that are hard to ignore.">

Its **offline-first planning with proactive reminders**. Students can plan and get called about what matters even with no internet. Everything else (chat, study tools, Ask USTP) builds on that reliable core.

</PracticeQuestion>

<PracticeQuestion q="What makes LAFINA different from Google Calendar or a normal alarm app?" tip="Proactive call + conversation + offline + review before saving.">

- **It is proactive.** Reminders arrive as a call you answer and respond to, not a silent banner.
- **It is conversational.** You can say "remind me to submit my lab report tomorrow at 5" or "give me ten more minutes".
- **It works offline.** Planning, alarms, and reminder calls work without internet.
- **It is a companion.** It also chats, helps you study, and answers USTP questions.

</PracticeQuestion>

<PracticeQuestion q="Why not just use ChatGPT or Google Assistant?" level="curveball" tip="They don't own your schedule offline or call you; LAFINA does both, safely.">

General assistants are online-only for most features, don't keep a student-focused planner on the phone, and don't deliver reminders as a call you have to acknowledge. LAFINA combines an offline planner, call-style reminders, and an assistant that can only change your data after you confirm. We do use a large online model (DeepSeek) for conversation, so we benefit from that intelligence without depending on it. Our goal isn't to beat ChatGPT at general intelligence. It's to be a focused companion for students.

</PracticeQuestion>

## 2. Offline and online

<PracticeQuestion q="Does LAFINA need the internet?" tip="Not for the core.">

Not for the core. Tasks, notes, calendar, alarms, reminder calls, small talk, supported voice commands, and local Ask USTP all work offline. Open conversation and trivia, AI-generated study material, online voice, documents, and sync need internet.

</PracticeQuestion>

<PracticeQuestion q="How does LAFINA work without the internet?" tip="Local database + local understanding + Android alarms.">

Data lives in a SQLite database on the phone, so saving never waits for a server. Commands are understood on the phone by a rule-based parser, plus an optional on-device model. Speech is transcribed on the phone with Whisper. Reminders are registered with Android's own alarm system, so they ring with no connection.

</PracticeQuestion>

<PracticeQuestion q="What is the difference between offline and online mode?" tip="Offline = core; online = smarter conversation, natural voice, sync.">

| Feature | Offline | Online Mode |
|---|---|---|
| Calendar, tasks, notes, alarms | ✅ | ✅ + sync |
| Reminder calls | ✅ phone voice | ✅ can continue as a live conversation |
| Planner commands | ✅ rules + on-device model | ✅ + DeepSeek |
| Conversation | Small talk only | Open conversation, trivia, study help |
| Ask USTP | Local search with sources | Friendlier answers from sources |
| Call LAFINA | Offline call (Whisper + phone TTS) | Live voice (ElevenLabs + DeepSeek) |

</PracticeQuestion>

<PracticeQuestion q="Why did you implement offline AI?" tip="Unreliable data; reminders must never depend on a signal.">

Not every student has steady mobile data or Wi-Fi, and a reminder that depends on the internet can fail exactly when it matters. Keeping essential functions on the phone makes LAFINA dependable and more private.

</PracticeQuestion>

<PracticeQuestion q="Is offline AI as intelligent as online AI?" tip="No, and we don't claim it is.">

No. On-device processing is limited by the phone's memory and speed. That's why offline LAFINA focuses on supported planning commands and small talk, and asks for Online Mode for general questions instead of guessing. For everyday planning, offline is enough.

</PracticeQuestion>

<PracticeQuestion q="Is LAFINA actually offline AI, or just hardcoded commands?" level="deeper" tip="Both, by design: rules for clear commands, a local model for messy ones.">

It's a deliberate combination:
- A deterministic **rule-based parser** handles clear commands instantly.
- When it isn't confident (below 0.85) or the phrasing is Taglish or casual, an **optional on-device language model** (SmolLM2, Gemma 3, or Qwen 2.5 via llama.cpp) reads the request.
- Speech recognition (Whisper) is a neural model running on the phone.

Using rules first makes it fast, predictable, and light on battery. We don't claim every offline command goes through a language model. We can demonstrate the difference live.

</PracticeQuestion>

<PracticeQuestion q="Does the offline AI require users to download anything?" tip="Rules built in; optional models downloaded once.">

The rule-based parser is built in ("Fast-Path Only"). The optional on-device models are downloaded once from the AI engine settings. They range from about 105 MB (SmolLM2) to 806 MB (Gemma 3 1B), and the app recommends one based on the phone's RAM. Offline speech also needs its speech model and an installed offline phone voice.

</PracticeQuestion>

<PracticeQuestion q="What if the user has no internet from the moment they install LAFINA?" level="deeper" tip="Guest mode works; downloads wait.">

They can use **guest mode** right away: planner, alarms, reminder calls, and the built-in parser need no account and no internet. Optional model downloads, sign-in, and online features wait until they connect.

</PracticeQuestion>

<PracticeQuestion q="What happens if the user loses internet while using LAFINA?" tip="Falls back; nothing local is lost.">

Online features fall back gracefully. Chat answers with an on-device reply labeled "Offline reply", and an online reminder call switches to on-device voice. Local data is untouched, and unsynced changes wait in the outbox until the connection returns.

</PracticeQuestion>

<PracticeQuestion q="Why not make everything completely offline?" tip="Phones can't run big models well; hybrid is the balance.">

Large AI models need far more memory, storage, and battery than most student phones have. Forcing open conversation or natural voice offline would make LAFINA slow or unusable on lower-end phones. A hybrid gives the best of both: a reliable offline core with optional online intelligence.

</PracticeQuestion>

<PracticeQuestion q="How can you call LAFINA a 'local' AI secretary if it uses cloud AI?" level="curveball" tip="'Local' = the essentials run on the phone; cloud is an add-on.">

"Local" refers to its essential functions running on the device: planning, reminders, calls, supported voice commands, and local Ask USTP. Cloud AI enhances LAFINA when available, but no core function requires it.

</PracticeQuestion>

## 3. Architecture and technology

<PracticeQuestion q="Describe the system architecture." tip="Phone (UI, services, SQLite, native) + optional backend + providers.">

There are two parts.

- **The Android app does the core work.** React Native and TypeScript screens sit on top of app services that understand requests, check them, and prepare review cards. One SQLite database on the phone holds all planner data. Native Kotlin and C++ code handles alarms, the microphone, Whisper speech recognition, and the optional on-device model.
- **An optional backend adds online features.** It is built with FastAPI, runs on Vercel, and stores accounts in Neon PostgreSQL. It handles sign-in, sync, and online AI, and connects to DeepSeek (chat), ElevenLabs (voice), and Pinecone (handbook search).

</PracticeQuestion>

<PracticeQuestion q="Why did you choose a hybrid architecture?" tip="Accessibility + capability.">

It balances accessibility and capability. Offline processing keeps the essentials available on any connection. Online services add advanced conversation and natural voice when available.

</PracticeQuestion>

<PracticeQuestion q="What programming languages and frameworks did you use?" tip="TypeScript + React Native; Kotlin + C++ native; Python backend.">

TypeScript with **React Native 0.86** for the app, **Kotlin** and **C/C++** (JNI) for native Android parts like alarms, Whisper, and the local model, and **Python FastAPI** for the backend.

</PracticeQuestion>

<PracticeQuestion q="Why React Native instead of native Android only?" level="deeper" tip="One TypeScript codebase for UI; native modules where needed.">

React Native lets us write the interface and most logic in TypeScript, which is faster to build and test. Where performance or Android features matter (alarms, audio, Whisper, the local model, OCR) we write native Kotlin and C++ modules and call them from the app.

</PracticeQuestion>

<PracticeQuestion q="Why did you prioritize Android?" tip="Users + features + scope.">

Most of our target students use Android phones. Android also allows the features LAFINA depends on (exact alarms, full-screen call screens over the lock screen, background audio), and focusing on one platform kept the scope manageable.

</PracticeQuestion>

<PracticeQuestion q="What does 'offline-first' mean in LAFINA?" tip="Local first, sync later.">

The phone holds the working copy of all data. Saving a task never waits for a server. Changes are written to SQLite first, and if the student is signed in they are queued and synced later when there is a connection.

</PracticeQuestion>

<PracticeQuestion q="What database does LAFINA use, and why?" tip="SQLite: embedded, offline, transactional.">

**SQLite** (through OP-SQLite), with schema version 28 and 36 tables. It's embedded, needs no server, works offline, and supports transactions. Its FTS5 full-text search powers offline handbook search. The cloud copy for synced accounts lives in Neon PostgreSQL.

</PracticeQuestion>

<PracticeQuestion q="Why do you need a backend if LAFINA works offline?" tip="Online-only jobs + keeping API keys secret.">

For things that can't happen on the phone alone: accounts, sync between phone and PC, online AI and voice, and documents. It also keeps provider **API keys on the server** so they're never inside the app.

</PracticeQuestion>

<PracticeQuestion q="How does sync work, and what happens to edits made offline?" level="deeper" tip="Outbox in the same transaction.">

Every change is written to its table **and** to a sync outbox in the same database transaction, so they can't get out of step. When the student is signed in and online, a sync worker sends the outbox to the backend, receives changes from other devices, and records any conflicts. Offline edits simply wait in the outbox. Seven record types sync: profile, tasks, events, time blocks, reminders, notes, and custom categories. Alarm clocks stay on the device.

</PracticeQuestion>

<PracticeQuestion q="Can you use LAFINA without an account?" tip="Guest mode.">

Yes. Guest mode works fully on the phone. An account adds sync and online features such as DeepSeek chat and online voice. When a guest signs up later, their data carries over once, without duplicates.

</PracticeQuestion>

<PracticeQuestion q="What are the minimum requirements for running LAFINA?" tip="Android 11+, 64-bit ARM; RAM depends on optional model.">

Android 11 (API 30) or newer on a 64-bit ARM phone. The core app is light. Optional on-device models need more: about 3 GB RAM for SmolLM2, 4 GB for Gemma 3 270M or Qwen 2.5, and 6 GB for Gemma 3 1B. *(Fill in: the actual phones you tested on.)*

</PracticeQuestion>

<PracticeQuestion q="Will LAFINA work on low-end smartphones?" tip="Yes for the core; pick a smaller model or none.">

Yes for the core. Low-end phones can use the built-in rule parser alone ("Fast-Path Only") or the smallest model. Larger on-device models may be slow or not recommended on phones with little RAM, and the app's hardware advisor suggests a suitable one.

</PracticeQuestion>

<PracticeQuestion q="Does LAFINA consume a lot of battery?" tip="Rules first, models only when needed, Android alarms not polling.">

We designed it to be light. Rules handle clear commands without running a model, reminders use Android's alarm system instead of a background timer, and the microphone only opens when you start voice. Voice conversations and on-device models use more battery while they run. *(Fill in: measured battery results, if any.)*

</PracticeQuestion>

## 4. Conversation and AI

<PracticeQuestion q="Walk us through what happens when a student sends a message." tip="Save → understand → route → reply.">

1. The message is saved to the chat history.
2. LAFINA works out the **intent**: what the student wants, plus details such as title, date, and time.
3. The intent is routed one of three ways:
   - **A change** (add, edit, or delete something) becomes a review card.
   - **A question about their plans** is answered by reading local data.
   - **General chat** goes to DeepSeek when online, or to a local reply when offline.
4. The reply is saved, shown, and can be read aloud.

</PracticeQuestion>

<PracticeQuestion q="How does LAFINA understand a request? Is it all an LLM?" tip="Cheapest first: rules → on-device model → DeepSeek. Always validated.">

No. LAFINA tries the cheapest method first.

1. **A rule-based "fast-path" parser** handles clear commands instantly.
2. **An optional on-device model** (via llama.cpp) is used if the parser is not confident (below 0.85) or the request is Taglish or casual.
3. **DeepSeek** can also read planner commands when Online Mode is on.

Whatever method is used, the result is validated before anything happens.

</PracticeQuestion>

<PracticeQuestion q="What AI technologies does LAFINA use?" tip="Whisper + VAD, fast-path + optional GGUF, phone TTS; online DeepSeek, ElevenLabs, Pinecone.">

**On the phone:**
- Whisper (`tiny.en`, quantized) with Silero VAD for speech recognition
- A rule-based parser, plus an optional GGUF model run through llama.cpp. The student can choose SmolLM2-135M, Gemma 3 270M, Qwen 2.5 0.5B, or Gemma 3 1B.
- The phone's built-in text-to-speech
- Small machine-learning models (EWMA, KNN, Random Forest) for adaptive reminder timing

**Online:**
- DeepSeek for replies
- ElevenLabs for voice
- Pinecone for handbook retrieval

</PracticeQuestion>

<PracticeQuestion q="Did you develop your own AI model?" tip="No. We integrated existing models; our work is the system around them.">

No. We integrated existing open-source models and online services. Our contribution is the system around them:
- the app and its workflows,
- offline command processing and intent routing,
- local storage and sync,
- reminder and call delivery,
- handbook retrieval,
- the safety rules that decide when AI may change data.

The small adaptive-timing models are implemented in the app itself.

</PracticeQuestion>

<PracticeQuestion q="Why did you not train your own AI model?" tip="Data, compute, time, cost; better to apply proven models.">

Training a language or speech model needs huge datasets, expensive computing power, and a lot of time. Existing models are already strong. We focused our effort on applying them well to the student problem and making them safe and reliable inside the app.

</PracticeQuestion>

<PracticeQuestion q="Why did you choose DeepSeek?" tip="Capable, affordable, good at structured output.">

DeepSeek handles flexible conversation and complex requests that lightweight offline methods can't, it supports tool calling for the Chat agent, and it's cost-effective for a student project. It's accessed through our backend, so it could be swapped for another provider. *(Fill in: any comparison your team did.)*

</PracticeQuestion>

<PracticeQuestion q="What is the purpose of ElevenLabs?" tip="Natural voice + live voice calls, online only.">

ElevenLabs provides natural-sounding speech and the real-time live voice used in the online Call LAFINA. Offline, LAFINA uses the phone's built-in voice instead.

</PracticeQuestion>

<PracticeQuestion q="Can LAFINA chit-chat and answer trivia? Does that work offline?" tip="Online: full conversation. Offline: small talk + planner only.">

Yes, with Online Mode: DeepSeek handles open conversation, trivia, explanations, and study help, and replies stream word by word.

Offline, LAFINA still handles small talk (greetings, "how are you", thanks, "what can you do") and all supported planner commands. For general-knowledge questions it says that Online Mode is needed. We don't pretend a tiny on-device model can answer trivia reliably.

</PracticeQuestion>

<PracticeQuestion q="What is agentic AI in LAFINA?" level="deeper" tip="AI that takes bounded actions through tools, ending in a card you confirm.">

Agentic AI means the assistant can take actions, not just write text. In LAFINA, the online **Chat agent** can call tools: look up your schedule, find free time, or prepare a change. It can do this over up to 4 steps. The tools run **on the phone**, and the result is a review card. The AI still can't save anything until you confirm.

</PracticeQuestion>

<PracticeQuestion q="What stops the AI from making a wrong or harmful change?" tip="Validation → review card → no model-only deletes.">

There are three safeguards:

1. Every intent is validated, and broken model output falls back to plain "chat", which never writes data.
2. Every planner change becomes a **review card** that the student must confirm.
3. Deleting is never prepared from the model's words alone.

A few small, easily undone actions (completing a task, snoozing or acknowledging a reminder, pinning a note) apply right away with an Undo option.

</PracticeQuestion>

<PracticeQuestion q="Can LAFINA make mistakes?" tip="Yes. Design catches them before they become data.">

Yes. Like any AI, it can misunderstand. That's why every interpretation is shown on a **review card** that the student can edit or cancel, low-risk actions have **Undo**, and Ask USTP shows its sources. Mistakes become corrections instead of wrong data.

</PracticeQuestion>

<PracticeQuestion q="What if the AI hallucinates a date or time?" level="deeper" tip="The student sees it on the card before it is saved.">

The review card shows the interpreted title, date, and time, plus any schedule conflict, before anything is saved. The student can edit or cancel. So a wrong reading becomes a correction, not a wrong reminder.

</PracticeQuestion>

<PracticeQuestion q="How do you prevent AI hallucinations?" tip="Ground answers in data/sources, validate actions, admit 'not sure'. Reduce, not eliminate.">

- For **school information**, answers come from retrieved handbook sources, and LAFINA says "I couldn't verify that" when sources don't support an answer.
- For **your plans**, answers are read from your actual database. DeepSeek is told to use only verified tool results.
- For **actions**, outputs are validated and confirmed on a card.

These steps reduce hallucinations. They can't eliminate them completely.

</PracticeQuestion>

<PracticeQuestion q="Can LAFINA answer everything?" tip="No. Clear limits.">

No. Online it can discuss most topics, but it can still be wrong and shouldn't be treated as an authority. Offline it only answers supported commands and small talk, and USTP answers are limited to its sources. It says so when it can't help.

</PracticeQuestion>

<PracticeQuestion q="What is the Chat agent?" level="deeper" tip="DeepSeek asks; tools run on the phone; max 4 steps; ends in a review card.">

When online, DeepSeek can use tools to look up the student's planner (for example, free time) over up to four steps and prepare one review card. The tools run **on the phone** against local data, and the server only relays. It still cannot save anything without the student's confirmation.

</PracticeQuestion>

<PracticeQuestion q="Why do you need AI for something as simple as a reminder?" level="curveball" tip="AI isn't for ringing; it's for understanding you.">

The reminder itself doesn't need AI: Android's alarm system rings it. The AI makes **creating and responding to** reminders natural. You can say "remind me to submit my lab report tomorrow at 5" instead of filling in several fields, and answer a call with "give me ten minutes". Manual forms are still there for anyone who prefers them.

</PracticeQuestion>

<PracticeQuestion q="What if the AI is slower than adding a schedule manually?" level="curveball" tip="Choice of input; rules are instant.">

For simple items, tapping can be just as fast, and that's fine because manual screens are always available. Clear commands also go through the instant rule parser, not a slow model. Voice and chat help most when your hands are busy or the request is complex.

</PracticeQuestion>

<PracticeQuestion q="What if we remove all AI services? Would LAFINA still be useful?" level="curveball" tip="Yes. The core is a full planner with call reminders.">

Yes. The calendar, tasks, notes, alarms, call-style reminders (with buttons), COR import, and local handbook search all work without any AI model. Removing AI would reduce how natural and conversational it is, but the core stays.

</PracticeQuestion>

<PracticeQuestion q="What if the user does not want to use AI?" tip="Manual everything; online AI is opt-in.">

They can use LAFINA as a normal planner with manual screens. Online AI is controlled by **Online Mode**, and Memory can be turned off.

</PracticeQuestion>

<PracticeQuestion q="Is LAFINA truly intelligent, or just following instructions?" level="curveball" tip="AI understands; controlled code acts. On purpose.">

Both, on purpose. AI is used to **understand** natural language and hold conversations. **Controlled, tested code** performs the actions on your schedule. Schedules and reminders must be reliable, so we never let a model freely change data.

</PracticeQuestion>

<PracticeQuestion q="How do you handle Taglish?" tip="Parser detects it and hands it to a model.">

For **typed** Taglish, the rule parser detects Taglish and casual scheduling phrases and doesn't trust its own reading. It hands them to the on-device model, or DeepSeek when online. The student still confirms the result on the review card.

</PracticeQuestion>

<PracticeQuestion q="What if the user speaks Bisaya, or mixes Bisaya and English?" level="curveball" tip="Honest: offline voice is English-only; typing and online help.">

Honestly, offline voice recognition uses Whisper `tiny.en`, an **English-only** model, so spoken Bisaya or Tagalog won't be recognized reliably offline. Typed mixed-language messages work better, especially online with DeepSeek, and the review card lets the student correct anything. Better local-language voice support is future work. *(Fill in: any mixed-language tests you ran.)*

</PracticeQuestion>

## 5. Reminders and calls

<PracticeQuestion q="How does LAFINA create schedules?" tip="Natural language → intent + slots → review card → SQLite.">

You can add items manually, import a COR, or just say or type a request. LAFINA extracts the activity, date, and time, shows them on a review card (with any conflicts), and saves after you confirm.

</PracticeQuestion>

<PracticeQuestion q="Can LAFINA create schedules without internet?" tip="Yes.">

Yes. Manual entry, COR import, and supported natural-language commands are all processed on the phone and saved to the local database.

</PracticeQuestion>

<PracticeQuestion q="Why did you choose call-based reminders?" tip="Alert fatigue; calls get answered (up to 39% fewer missed appointments).">

Students receive many notifications a day and learn to ignore them (alert fatigue). People are conditioned to answer calls, and healthcare studies found call reminders reduce missed appointments by up to 39%. A call also asks the student to respond, so they actually process the reminder.

</PracticeQuestion>

<PracticeQuestion q="How does a reminder call work?" tip="AlarmManager → checks → ring → speak → listen → acknowledge/snooze.">

1. Android's `AlarmManager` fires at the reminder time, even if the app is closed.
2. LAFINA checks the reminder is still valid: right owner, still pending, task not already done.
3. A full-screen call appears, including over the lock screen.
4. When the student answers, LAFINA speaks the reminder and listens with Whisper on the phone.
5. "Okay" or "done" acknowledges the reminder. "Give me ten minutes" snoozes it for that long.
6. Buttons are always available as a fallback.

</PracticeQuestion>

<PracticeQuestion q="Is it a real phone call?" tip="No. Simulated, in-app, offline.">

No. It is a simulated call screen inside LAFINA. It uses no phone number or carrier, which is why it works offline and costs nothing.

</PracticeQuestion>

<PracticeQuestion q="Does receiving a reminder call consume mobile load?" tip="No.">

No. It's a screen inside the app, not a cellular call. Only online features (like a live online voice conversation) use internet data, and never call credits.

</PracticeQuestion>

<PracticeQuestion q="What is Call LAFINA, and how is it different from a reminder call?" tip="Student starts it; online or offline mode picked at start.">

A reminder call is started **by LAFINA** when something is due. Call LAFINA is started **by the student** to talk hands-free.

When the call starts, LAFINA picks a mode:
- **Online** if the student is signed in, Online Mode is on, and there is internet. This uses ElevenLabs live voice with DeepSeek.
- **Offline** otherwise. This uses Whisper, the local parser, and the phone's voice.

A running call never switches modes by itself.

</PracticeQuestion>

<PracticeQuestion q="What if the student declines, doesn't answer, or isn't understood?" tip="Auto-snooze; after the limit, 'missed'.">

## 6. Ask USTP, RAG and study tools

<PracticeQuestion q="What is RAG and how does Ask USTP use it?" tip="Retrieve sources first, then answer from them, with citations.">

RAG stands for Retrieval-Augmented Generation. LAFINA first retrieves relevant passages from the USTP handbook and its knowledge pack, then answers from those passages and shows the sources. Offline, it searches the local pack using SQLite full-text search. Online, DeepSeek writes a friendlier answer from the retrieved evidence. If the sources don't support an answer, LAFINA says it couldn't verify it instead of guessing.

</PracticeQuestion>

<PracticeQuestion q="Why did you implement RAG?" tip="General AI doesn't know USTP policy.">

General AI models don't reliably know specific USTP policies and may make them up. RAG makes answers come from **actual USTP documents**, with sources students can check.

</PracticeQuestion>

<PracticeQuestion q="Is RAG the same as training an AI?" tip="No: retrieval feeds documents at answer time; training changes the model.">

No. Training changes what a model has learned internally. RAG leaves the model unchanged and **gives it the relevant passages at the moment it answers**. That's why updating LAFINA's knowledge just means updating its documents.

</PracticeQuestion>

<PracticeQuestion q="Can LAFINA retrieve handbook information offline?" tip="Yes: local pack + FTS5.">

Yes. The **2023 USTP Student Handbook** and a **USTP CDO knowledge snapshot** are bundled and searched on the phone with SQLite FTS5 full-text search. Offline answers show the matching excerpts and their sources. Online answers are more conversational.

</PracticeQuestion>

<PracticeQuestion q="Where does LAFINA get its academic information?" tip="Handbook 2023 + CDO snapshot (official pages).">

From the **USTP Student Handbook (2023 edition)** and a **USTP Cagayan de Oro knowledge snapshot** of official pages: the AY 2026–2027 academic calendar, offices and contacts, portals such as PRISMS, the library, programs, scholarships, and organizations. Every answer lists the sources it used.

<PracticeQuestion q="What if the handbook becomes outdated?" tip="Update the documents; LAFINA names the edition.">

<PracticeQuestion q="How can students trust the information provided?" tip="Sources shown + 'couldn't verify' + verify with official offices.">

Every answer shows its sources (pages or official links), so students can check the exact passage. When the sources don't support an answer, LAFINA says it couldn't verify it. For important decisions, students should still confirm with the Registrar or the Office of Student Affairs.

## 7. Memory and personalization

<PracticeQuestion q="How does LAFINA remember things about the student?" level="deeper" tip="Memory is visible, editable, and filtered for secrets.">

Students can say things like "I'm a night owl" or "call me Jo", and LAFINA saves them to **Memory**. These preferences are used for study scheduling and the Morning Briefing. Students can ask "what do you remember about me?", forget items, or turn Memory off. Passwords, PINs, OTPs, card numbers, and bank or GCash numbers are blocked from being saved. Temporary Chat saves no memories.

</PracticeQuestion>

<PracticeQuestion q="What is the difference between Memory and chat history?" tip="History = what was said; Memory = what's worth keeping.">

Chat history is the record of past conversations. Memory is a small set of **selected facts and preferences** (name, study habits, goals) that LAFINA keeps and uses across all chats, calls, and briefings.

</PracticeQuestion>

<PracticeQuestion q="Does LAFINA get smarter the more you use it?" tip="More personal, not retrained.">

It becomes more **personal**, not retrained. It uses stored preferences (Memory) and your response history for adaptive reminder timing. The underlying language models don't change.

</PracticeQuestion>

<PracticeQuestion q="Can LAFINA remember incorrect information?" tip="Yes, and you can see, forget, or undo it.">

Yes, it can misunderstand. That's why Memory is visible and controllable: ask "what do you remember about me?", say "forget that…", use Undo, or turn Memory off.

</PracticeQuestion>

<PracticeQuestion q="Why is personalization important?" tip="Every student's schedule and habits differ.">

Every student has different classes, habits, and goals. Knowing that someone studies late or prefers a certain name lets LAFINA suggest better study times, give a relevant Morning Briefing, and time reminders better.

</PracticeQuestion>

<PracticeQuestion q="What if two users use the same phone?" level="deeper" tip="Account-scoped data; switching cancels in-flight work; guest is shared.">

Data is scoped to the signed-in account: chats, memories, sync, and calls belong to their owner, and switching accounts cancels any work in progress so nothing leaks between users. The guest profile is one shared local identity, so people sharing a phone should each use their own account.

</PracticeQuestion>

<PracticeQuestion q="Can LAFINA predict a student's academic performance?" level="curveball" tip="No. Its ML only predicts reminder timing.">

No. LAFINA's on-device machine learning only predicts **reminder timing**: when a student is likely to respond and whether a call or a notification works better. Predicting grades would need different data and a separate study.

## Recommended opening answer

If the panel asks, **"Can you briefly explain what your capstone is all about?"**:

> LAFINA, or **Local AI Secretary**, is an AI student companion for Android that helps students manage their academic life.
>
> Students can manage their classes, tasks, reminders, and notes by tapping, typing, or talking. When something important is due, LAFINA doesn't just send a notification. It calls them with a simulated phone call they answer and respond to.
>
> What makes LAFINA different is its **offline-first** design: planning, reminders, and supported voice commands work without internet. With Online Mode, it becomes a full companion that chats, answers questions, helps with studying, and answers USTP questions with sources.
>
> And whatever the AI suggests, the student stays in control: every change is shown for confirmation before it's saved.
